Sunsetting AetherOS — and introducing DreamyOS

Today we're sharing one of the biggest decisions in the history of our studio — a decision that was neither easy nor made quickly. After a long period of evaluation, we have decided to close the AetherOS project.

Why we're closing AetherOS

AetherOS started as an ambitious experiment: our attempt to build a complete operating system experience as a small, independent team. We learned an enormous amount from it — about systems architecture, tooling, and what it truly means to ship software at that scale.

But over time, we came to a difficult conclusion: AetherOS, in its current form, was built on a foundation we don't fully own. Its direction depended on the choices of other projects, and every step forward required working around limitations that were never ours to begin with. Rather than keep patching that foundation, we chose to build the one we actually want.

We won't be releasing further AetherOS updates. The repository will remain public as an archive, so the work we put into it isn't lost — it remains a record of where we started.

Introducing DreamyOS

With AetherOS behind us, we're excited to announce what comes next: DreamyOS — our new operating system project, and the most ambitious thing DreamyByte has ever attempted.

DreamyOS is designed from day one to be truly ours. Where AetherOS leaned on existing components, DreamyOS is being built on a completely independent foundation:

  • Our own kernel — a from-scratch kernel designed around the architecture decisions we actually want, not compromises we inherited.
  • Our own desktop environment (DE) — a custom DE with a cohesive visual language, designed and implemented by us, delivering the dreamy, polished experience DreamyByte is known for.
  • Our own toolchain and drivers — the system utilities, drivers, and core userland grown in-house, so every layer of the stack is something we understand end to end.

Our own kernel, our own DE, our own identity — DreamyOS is what AetherOS was always meant to become, rebuilt without the walls.

What happens next

Development is starting now. Because we're building the foundation first, progress will initially look slow: kernels, boot, and system services turn into visible results only after a lot of invisible work. We'll share our progress in public — through our Changelog and the links you'll find there — and we'll be honest about both wins and setbacks.

To everyone who followed AetherOS: thank you. Everything we learned from the project flows directly into DreamyOS. This isn't the end of a story — it's the start of a much bigger one.

— The DreamyByte Team
